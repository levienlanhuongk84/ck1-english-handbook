Paper Descriptions

Paper 1 · Reading

40 marks4 passages60 min

Texts

  • Informational texts of increasing length — general press, educational magazines, descriptive and narrative writing
  • Everyday, academic and workplace topics; unfamiliar vocabulary ≤ 10% at B1

Question types

  • Main purpose of the passage or paragraph
  • Stated detail; meaning of words in context
  • Reference ("this" refers to…); inference

Official sample — Passage 1 (excerpt)

Digital Learning in Modern Education

In recent decades, digital technology has become a central part of how people learn. Schools and universities increasingly rely on online platforms, multimedia resources, and interactive tools to support teaching and learning. While traditional classrooms remain important, technology has expanded access to information and created new opportunities for students to develop essential skills.

Sample questions

1. What is the main purpose of the passage?

  • A. To show how digital learning helps teachers work less
  • B. To explain how technology is reshaping modern education
  • C. To argue that online schools are more effective
  • D. To describe the history of digital tools

3. The word "access" in paragraph 3 most nearly means:

  • A. refusal
  • B. arrival
  • C. entry
  • D. difficulty

Paper 2 · Writing

30 marks2 tasks60 min

Task 1 · Email or letter — 10 marks, approx. 20 min

  • Address all 3 required points; minimum 120 words; no names or addresses
Sample 1An exchange student asks about the computer lab, after-school activities and what you like about your school. Write a reply covering all 3 points.
Sample 2Your friend Robin invites you to a sports camp in the mountains and asks whether you'd prefer a room or a tent, what you could cook from your country, and what to do before the camp. Write a reply covering all 3 points.

Task 2 · Opinion essay — 20 marks, approx. 40 min

  • General social or academic topic; argument, examples and clear structure; minimum 250 words
Sample 1More and more people are moving from the countryside to live in cities. What problems can this cause? How can these problems be solved?
Sample 2Some people believe that university students should be required to attend classes. Others believe that going to classes should be optional for students. Say which view you agree with.

Assessment criteria

Task Fulfilment · Organisation · Vocabulary · Grammar.

Paper 3 · Listening

35 marks3 parts35–40 min

3 parts

  • P1 (items 1–8, 8 marks): 8 short announcements or instructions, 1 item each
  • P2 (items 9–20, 12 marks): 3 conversations × 4 items — turn-taking, speaker intention
  • P3 (items 21–35, 15 marks): 3 talks × 5 items — main ideas, supporting points, inference

Procedure

  • 5 minutes of instructions; each recording is played once while candidates answer
  • Slow to moderate speed; unfamiliar vocabulary ≤ 5%

Paper 4 · Speaking

10 marks3 parts10–12 min

Procedure

  • Draw 1 of 10 B1 topics; 5–7 minutes' preparation
  • P1 · Social interaction (3 marks, 2–3 min): questions on 2 everyday topics
  • P2 · Topic development (5 marks, 4–5 min): present the drawn topic using the prompt diagram
  • P3 · Extended questions (2 marks, 3–4 min): follow-up and challenge questions

Official sample — Speaking Test No. 1

Part 1 · Social interaction

Let's talk about food.

  • What kind of food do you like?
  • Do you prefer eating at home or eating out? Why?
  • Is it important to eat healthy food?

Let's talk about free time.

  • What do you usually do in your free time?
  • Do you prefer spending your free time alone or with friends? Why?
  • Do you think people today have enough free time?

Topic: Online learning is becoming popular.

Prompt diagram:

  • Your own ideas
Part 3 · Extended questions
  • Why do many people choose online learning today?
  • Is online learning suitable for all students? Why or why not?
  • Should schools combine online and face-to-face learning?

Learning Outcomes, Eligibility and Grading

The course is completed on meeting the B1 learning outcomes and achieving a converted result of 5.0 or above.

Learning Outcomes

1 · Knowledge

B1 grammar and vocabulary

Outcomes 1.1–1.12 correspond to the 12 taught units — see Sheet 2.

2 · Skills

4 skills at independent-user level

  • Listening: main ideas, key words, detail; follow short conversations
  • Speaking: unprepared conversation; prepared presentation
  • Reading: skimming, main ideas; texts of 3–5 paragraphs
  • Writing: letters and short texts in standard formats
3 · Attitude

Initiative and integrity

Active use of English; academic integrity; self-directed improvement.

Eligibility & Grading

Eligibility

  • Required class attendance
  • All 4 papers taken, unless exempt

Conversion formula

Result = Total marks × 10 ÷ 115

Round down to 1 decimal place. To pass: the result must be 5.0 or higher, and each paper must reach at least 30% of its own maximum marks.
